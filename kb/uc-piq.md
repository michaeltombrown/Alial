# UC Personal Insight Question (PIQ)

## The essay
- University of California first-year applicants answer four of eight Personal Insight Questions, each capped at 350 words. The topics are: a leadership experience; creativity; a talent or skill; an educational opportunity or barrier overcome; a significant challenge; a favorite academic subject and how the student pursued it; a contribution to a community; and what sets the student apart. Questions and rules can change, so use the question the student supplies.
- PIQs are not formal essays. They are direct answers that readers use alongside the rest of the application. No title, introduction ritual, or conclusion flourish is needed.
- The "additional comments" box is separate and is for context, not another essay.

## What strong responses do
- Answer the question that was asked, early, in plain language.
- Use concrete examples with specifics: what the student did, how, with whom, and what changed.
- Show personal contribution and reflection within the word limit, in the student's own voice.
- Choose a distinct angle for each of the four responses so they add new information.

## Common weaknesses
- A slow, story-style opening that delays the answer until late in 350 words.
- Vague claims about leadership or creativity with no specific action.
- Repeating the activities list or another PIQ.
- Answering a different question than the one chosen, or squeezing in so many examples that none is developed.
- Impressive-sounding language in place of plain, specific description.

## How to calibrate the analysis
- Weight "Prompt Fit" and directness heavily. A hook and a polished ending matter less than a clear, specific answer.
- Judge the response on its own and note how it would sit alongside three others. Do not penalize the lack of a full narrative arc.
- Check against 350 words and flag anything far over or very short.
- Hardship-focused questions (challenge, barrier) call for growth and agency. Other questions do not.

## Suggestions worth offering
- Ask the student to name the question in their first sentence's job: does it answer it?
- Suggest replacing broad statements with the two or three specifics that prove them.
- Suggest trimming context to leave room for contribution and reflection.
- Offer unrelated-scenario illustrations. Never write the student's answer.
