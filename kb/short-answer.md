# Supplemental: Short Answer / Quirky

## The essay
- Brief responses, often 50 to 250 words, sometimes playful ("what is your favorite…", "what would you teach…"). They reveal personality, curiosity, humor, and how the student thinks. Use the prompt the student supplies.
- The reader is looking for a distinct voice and one memorable idea, delivered economically.

## What strong responses do
- Answer the actual question first, then add a surprising, specific detail or thought.
- Let humor and curiosity come naturally from the student, never from forced cleverness.
- Make each word do work: precise nouns and verbs, no throat-clearing.
- Feel different from the student's other essays by adding new information about them.

## Common weaknesses
- Clever concept with no substance behind it.
- Generic or safe answers any applicant might give.
- Missing the question or answering a different one.
- Padding to reach a word count, or cramming too many ideas into a short space.
- Repeating material from the main essay or activities.

## How to calibrate the analysis
- Treat it as a miniature. Weight originality, specificity, voice, and prompt fit. Hook and ending craft matter only in proportion to length.
- Hardship and long-arc growth categories usually do not apply. Mark them not applicable.
- Humor is valid when it reveals the student. Do not mistake joke density for quality.
- Check the word limit strictly.

## Suggestions worth offering
- Ask what small, specific thing the student would only say in this answer.
- Suggest cutting the first sentence if it only warms up.
- Suggest trading an abstract claim for one vivid example.
- Illustrate with unrelated scenarios and never supply replacement wording.
