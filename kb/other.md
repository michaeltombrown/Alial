# Other Application Essay

## The essay
- Covers any essay or written response not listed elsewhere: institution-specific prompts, optional essays, and the "additional information" or "additional comments" boxes. Use the prompt and context the student supplies, and decide from them what kind of writing this is.
- Additional information or comments boxes are for context, not a second personal statement. Limits are typically short (a few hundred words), so check the form.

## What strong responses do
- Respond precisely to whatever was asked, in the requested length and tone.
- For contextual boxes: state circumstances factually and briefly (a disruption, an unusual schedule, a grade change), say what changed or how the student responded, and stop.
- For optional or institution-specific essays: offer genuinely new information about the student rather than repeating other parts of the application.
- Stay calm, specific, and respectful in tone.

## Common weaknesses
- Treating a context box as another essay, or repeating the main essay.
- Excuses, blame, or over-explanation.
- Missing the actual question.
- Padding because the space exists. An optional box does not have to be filled.
- Unverifiable claims or sensitive details the student did not need to share.

## How to calibrate the analysis
- First identify the type of response from the prompt or context. If unclear, say what you assumed.
- For context boxes, judge clarity, factual tone, and relevance. Craft categories such as hook and imagery do not apply. Mark them not applicable.
- For essays, apply the usual standards in proportion to length and purpose.
- Do not push the student to disclose private or painful information.

## Suggestions worth offering
- Ask whether the response gives the reader information that is not elsewhere in the application.
- Suggest cutting anything that sounds defensive or repeats other sections.
- Suggest leading with the fact the reader most needs.
- Never write the response for the student or invent details.
- Exercise to try: identify the question a reader would still have, and draft only the factual explanation needed to answer it. An additional-information box is not a required second personal statement.
