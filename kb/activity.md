# Supplemental: Activity or Extracurricular

## The essay
- A short response, often about 150 words, asking the student to elaborate on an activity, job, family responsibility, or commitment. Common App activity list entries are separate and much shorter. Use the prompt the student supplies.
- Do not confuse list limits with essay limits. On the Common App activities list the position, organization, and description fields allow 50, 100, and 150 characters. Those are character limits for list entries, not word limits for an activity essay.
- Jobs, family responsibilities, clubs, hobbies, volunteering, and sports all count as meaningful pursuits, and choosing meaningful activities beats filling every slot.
- Readers want to know why the activity matters to the student and what the student actually did and learned, beyond the title and hours.

## What strong responses do
- Focus on one memorable idea, moment, or contribution, not a summary of years of involvement.
- Make the student's own actions concrete: what they decided, built, taught, or changed.
- Include brief, honest reflection on why it matters or what it revealed.
- Use plain, specific language. Every sentence adds new information.

## Common weaknesses
- A list of roles, awards, and responsibilities that the activities section already contains.
- Vague claims of leadership, dedication, or passion without specifics.
- Trying to cover the whole activity and covering none of it well.
- Repeating the main essay.
- Filler openings and closing slogans that use up scarce words.

## How to calibrate the analysis
- Judge the response as a miniature, not a full essay. Hook, imagery, ending, and life-thread categories matter only lightly. Score them proportionally and say so.
- Growth from hardship is not applicable unless the student raises it.
- Check the word limit carefully. Even small overages matter at this length.
- Prioritize specificity, authenticity, and a single clear idea.

## Suggestions worth offering
- Ask what one thing the student did that no one else in the group did.
- Suggest cutting everything that restates the activity description.
- Suggest swapping a general statement for one concrete example.
- Illustrate with unrelated scenarios. Never write the student's text.
- Exercise to try: ask what the student personally did, why they kept going, and what they or others gained.
