# Supplemental: Why This College

## The essay
- A school-specific essay, often 100 to 400 words, asking why the student wants to attend or study a particular major. Readers use it to judge genuine interest and fit, and to see that the student has done real research.
- Limits and phrasing vary by school. Use the prompt and college the student supplies.

## What strong essays do
- Name specific, verifiable features of the school (programs, courses, research, advising structure, traditions, community practices) and connect each to something the student has actually done, asked, or wants to explore.
- Show how the student would use those opportunities, not just that they exist.
- Reveal motivation and personality in a few sentences, not a brochure's worth of praise.
- Stay concise. One or two well-developed connections beat a long list.

## Common weaknesses
- Generic praise (reputation, rankings, location, weather, "diverse community") that could describe any school.
- The swap test fails: replacing the school's name with another would leave the essay intact.
- Lists of programs with no link to the student's experience.
- Restating the school's own marketing language.
- Spending most of the space on the student's autobiography and little on the school.

## How to calibrate the analysis
- The main test is specificity and authentic fit, not literary polish. Hook and ending craft matter little at this length.
- If the college is not named, say you cannot check accuracy of school details and assess only specificity and fit logic.
- Do not invent facts about a school. If unsure a program or offering exists, tell the student to verify it on the school's official site.
- Do not apply growth-from-hardship expectations.

## Suggestions worth offering
- Ask for the one experience in the student's life that most explains the interest.
- Suggest pairing each school detail with a specific student action or question.
- Suggest cutting rankings and generic adjectives.
- Remind the student to verify every school-specific detail. Never write the essay.
