# Transfer Essay

## The essay
- A statement from a student leaving one college (or program) for another, usually 250 to 650 words. Some systems, such as the University of California, use a separate set of questions for transfer applicants, so use the prompt the student supplies.
- Readers look for a clear, honest reason for transferring, evidence of academic growth, readiness for the intended major, and specific fit with the new institution.

## What strong essays do
- Give an honest, forward-looking reason for the move that centers on the student's goals and what they learned, not on criticizing the current school.
- Show academic trajectory: courses, projects, research, or work that built readiness for the intended field.
- Connect specific offerings at the new school to the student's next steps.
- Address any gaps, grade dips, or disruptions briefly and factually, with what changed since.

## Common weaknesses
- Complaints about the current school or vague dissatisfaction.
- Generic praise for the new school that could apply to any campus.
- A list of courses and grades with no reflection.
- Excuses in place of explanation.
- Repeating the first-year application essay without updating it.

## How to calibrate the analysis
- Weight clarity of motivation, academic readiness, and specific fit over narrative flourish.
- Assess honesty and maturity of tone. Do not require a dramatic story.
- If a major is stated, check that the essay shows real engagement with it.
- Do not invent facts about either institution. Tell the student to verify school-specific details.

## Suggestions worth offering
- Ask what the student can now do or understand that they could not a year ago.
- Suggest replacing criticism with a positive statement of what they are seeking.
- Suggest linking each school detail to something already attempted.
- Illustrate with unrelated scenarios and never write the student's text.
