# Common App Personal Statement

## The essay
- One personal statement of 250 to 650 words goes to every school on the Common App. The student picks one of seven prompts or writes on any topic. The prompts have long covered: a background, identity, interest, or talent so meaningful the application would be incomplete without it; a setback or failure and what it taught; a time the student questioned or challenged a belief; something that prompted gratitude or surprise; an accomplishment or realization that sparked personal growth; a topic so engaging the student loses track of time; and a topic of the student's choice. No prompt is favored. Prompt wording changes by cycle and was not verified for this knowledge base, so never assert the text of a current prompt. Use the one the student supplies.
- Because it goes to every school, it should reveal the student, not tailor itself to one college.

## What strong essays do
- Show a real person thinking: specific scenes and details, then reflection that explains why the moment matters and what it reveals about values, curiosity, or character.
- Choose a focused moment, habit, or question over a survey of achievements. Ordinary topics work when the interpretation is distinctive.
- Sound like the student. Natural, specific language beats polished but generic prose.
- Reach an ending that feels earned and adds insight beyond summarizing the story.

## Common weaknesses
- Résumé in paragraph form, or a list of accomplishments the activities section already covers.
- Telling traits ("I am resilient") instead of showing choices and actions.
- Reflection that stays generic ("this taught me the value of hard work") or an ending that announces a lesson the story did not earn.
- Over-dramatized events, borrowed profundity, or a clever concept with no personal payoff.
- Writing that could belong to almost anyone.

## How to calibrate the analysis
- Judge fit to the supplied prompt, but accept that many strong essays fit several prompts. If no prompt is supplied, treat it as a prompt 7 style essay and say so.
- Hardship is not required and is not automatically more compelling. Do not penalize essays without it.
- Check word count against 650. Flag essays far under 250 or over 650.
- All of the craft categories (hook, imagery, ending, themes) apply fully to this essay type.

## Suggestions worth offering
- Point to the single moment that deserves more scene and less summary.
- Ask what the student noticed, decided, or got wrong in that moment.
- Suggest cutting any sentence that restates the activities list.
- Offer technique illustrations from unrelated scenarios and never rewrite the student's text.
- Exercise to try: ask the student to name one choice, observation, or contradiction that shows how they think, and to give one precise real example of it.
- Never presume a student's income, eligibility, or background from the essay.
